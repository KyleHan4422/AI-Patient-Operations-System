Who can see your dental records, how to get a copy, and how we treat your
information.

## Requesting Your Records

You can ask for a copy of your own record at any time. Requests are made in
writing, in person or by email, and we confirm your identity before releasing
anything. Copies are normally ready within a few working days; if you need them
sooner because of an appointment elsewhere, say so and we will prioritise it.

Records include your chart notes, treatment history and X-ray images. X-rays
are sent as digital files unless you ask for prints.

## Sending Records to Another Practice

Sign a release and we will send your records directly to the practice you name,
which is faster than carrying them yourself and avoids format problems with
X-ray images. There is no charge for a transfer to another dental practice.

You do not need to give a reason for transferring, and it does not affect how
you are treated here in the meantime.

## Who Can Ask About Your Care

We discuss your care with you. We will not confirm to anyone else that you are
a patient here, including family members, unless you have given us permission
in writing or are present and agree in the moment.

For children, the parent or legal guardian who consented to treatment may
discuss the child's care. Where guardianship is shared or disputed, we follow
the documentation on file and will ask for a court order if the situation is
unclear.

For adults who need help managing their care, we can record a named person on
your file who is allowed to book appointments and ask questions on your behalf.

## How We Use Your Information

Your information is used to provide care, to bill your insurer if you ask us
to, and to run the practice. We do not sell it and we do not use it for
advertising.

Appointment reminders are sent by text or email, whichever you choose. You can
turn them off at any time, and we will still call you if something needs your
attention.

## Corrections

If something in your record is wrong, tell us. Factual errors, such as a
misspelled name, a wrong date of birth or an incorrect allergy, are corrected
directly. Clinical notes are not rewritten after the fact; instead, your
comment is added to the record alongside the original entry, which is the
standard way to keep a medical record honest.
