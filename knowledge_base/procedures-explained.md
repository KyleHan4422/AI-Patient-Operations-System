Plain descriptions of the treatments we do most often: what happens, how many
visits it takes, and what it feels like. How long each appointment is booked
for, and what each costs, are held in our scheduling and fee records rather
than here.

## Examinations and X-Rays

An examination is a look at the teeth, gums, bite and soft tissues, plus a
check for signs of oral cancer. X-rays are taken when they will change a
decision: to see between teeth, below the gum line, or around the roots.

Modern dental X-rays use a very low dose. We do not take them on a fixed
schedule; we take them when there is a question they can answer.

## Cleanings

A routine cleaning removes plaque and hardened deposits, then polishes the
teeth. Most people feel scraping and vibration but no pain.

If there is inflammation or deep pockets between the gum and the tooth, a
deeper cleaning below the gum line may be recommended instead. That is a
different treatment, usually done with local anaesthetic and often split across
two visits, one for each side of the mouth.

## Fillings

A filling repairs a tooth damaged by decay. The decayed part is removed, the
space is cleaned and then filled with a tooth-coloured composite material that
is hardened with a light. One tooth normally takes one visit.

The area is numbed first. The numbness wears off over two to three hours.

## Crowns

A crown is a cap that covers a whole tooth, used when too much of the tooth is
missing or cracked for a filling to hold.

It normally takes two visits. At the first, the tooth is shaped, an impression
or scan is taken, and a temporary crown is fitted. The permanent crown is made
in a laboratory and fitted at the second visit, usually two to three weeks
later. Some practices make crowns in-house in a single visit; we do not.

## Root Canals

A root canal treats infection inside a tooth. The inflamed tissue is removed,
the canals are cleaned and sealed, and the tooth is then usually restored with
a crown, because a treated tooth is more brittle.

It is done under local anaesthetic. The reputation of root canals comes from an
era before modern anaesthesia; most patients say it feels similar to having a
filling, just longer. The relief afterwards is the part people remember.

## Extractions

A simple extraction removes a tooth that is visible in the mouth, under local
anaesthetic, usually in a single short visit. Teeth that are broken at the gum
line or impacted are a surgical extraction, and we refer those to an oral
surgeon.

We will always discuss whether the tooth can be saved before removing it.

## Whitening

In-office whitening lightens the natural shade of teeth using a gel applied
under supervision. It does not change the colour of fillings, crowns or
veneers, which is worth knowing before treating front teeth.

Results vary with the cause of the staining, and sensitivity for a day or two
afterwards is common and temporary.
