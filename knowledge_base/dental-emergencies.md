What counts as urgent, what to do in the first few minutes, and when a dental
practice is the wrong place to go.

## Go to a Hospital Emergency Room

Some situations are beyond what any dental office can treat safely. Go to an
emergency room, or call emergency services, if there is:

- difficulty breathing or swallowing, or swelling spreading toward the eye or
  down the neck;
- bleeding that will not stop with firm, continuous pressure;
- a serious injury to the face or jaw, or a head injury with loss of
  consciousness;
- chest pain, or pain spreading into the arm or jaw with sweating or shortness
  of breath.

Facial swelling with a fever is not something to sleep on. Infection that
tracks into the tissues of the floor of the mouth becomes dangerous quickly.

## A Knocked-Out Adult Tooth

This is the one true dental emergency where minutes matter. Pick the tooth up by
the crown, never the root. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or saline,
not tap water, and do not scrub it.

If you can, place it back into the socket and bite gently on a cloth to hold it.
If you cannot, keep it in milk, or in the patient's own saliva, and get to us
immediately. A tooth replanted within the first hour has a far better chance.

Baby teeth are not replanted, because doing so can damage the adult tooth
forming above. Still call us.

## A Cracked or Broken Tooth

Rinse with warm water and use a cold compress on the outside of the cheek.
Save any pieces. Avoid chewing on that side and call us the same day: a crack
that is only in enamel and one that reaches the nerve look similar from the
outside and are treated very differently.

## Toothache

Rinse with warm salt water and floss gently around the tooth to remove anything
trapped. Over-the-counter pain relief that you have taken safely before is
reasonable in the meantime.

Never hold an aspirin against the gum: it burns the tissue and does not help
the tooth.

Call us for any toothache that wakes you at night, is triggered by heat, or
comes with a bad taste or swelling. Those point to infection rather than
sensitivity.

## Lost Filling or Crown

Not an emergency, but do not leave it. Keep the crown if you have it, avoid
chewing on that side, and call for the next available appointment. Dental
cement sold in pharmacies is acceptable as a temporary measure for a day or
two; superglue is not, ever.

## Outside Opening Hours

Our phone line gives instructions for urgent problems outside working hours,
including when to go to an emergency room. Leave a message with a callback
number and what happened, and we will reach you as early as we can. If the
situation is on the list at the top of this page, do not wait for us.
