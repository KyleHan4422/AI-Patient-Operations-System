Everything a patient needs to know before their first appointment at Riverside
Family Dental. Booking, insurance and payment have their own pages.

## Paperwork Before You Arrive

New patients complete a health history form and a consent form. We email both
when the appointment is booked, and they can be filled in on a phone. Sending
them back the day before saves about fifteen minutes in the waiting room.

If a form is not completed in advance, come a little earlier and finish it at
the front desk. Nobody is turned away for missing paperwork.

## What to Bring

Bring a photo ID, your dental insurance card if you have one, and a list of any
medicines you take, including vitamins and supplements. If you have had dental
work elsewhere in the past two years, bring the name and phone number of that
practice so we can request records.

If you are being seen for a specific problem, it helps to bring anything that
came from another provider about it: a referral note, a prescription label, or
photos of a tooth that broke.

## Arriving for Your First Appointment

Arrive ten minutes before the appointment time. A first visit usually includes
a full examination, X-rays if they are needed, and a conversation about a
treatment plan. A cleaning is often done at the same visit, but not always: if
the examination shows that a deeper cleaning is appropriate, it will be booked
separately so enough time can be set aside for it.

Plan for a first visit to take longer than a routine check-up.

## Bringing a Child

We see children. A parent or legal guardian must attend the first visit and
sign the consent form; after that, an adult who is not the guardian may bring
the child if we have written permission on file.

Most children do well with a short, friendly first visit that is mainly about
meeting the hygienist and counting teeth. Tell us when you book if your child
is anxious or has had a difficult experience elsewhere, and we will plan more
time.

## Transferring From Another Dentist

You do not need to close your file with another practice before coming here.
Sign a records release at your first visit and we will request your chart and
recent X-rays, which often means we can avoid repeating images you have already
had taken.
